# B2 Loose Hours

Map: `b02.svg`. Ticket: #29. Level script: `levels/beginner/b02.py`. Shared numbers and assumptions: `README.md`.

The map is the approved design. "As built" at the end lists where the level script differs from it, and why.

## Intent

The second level teaches jumping and checkpoints, with trapdoors and crumbling tiles. The player learns each one in a safe place, then crosses a wide hall where the floor is the puzzle. The hall has no fixed path. Slow players go round on solid panels. Fast players go straight across the loose ones.

Flavour: a clock workshop floor with brass hatches, some of them worn loose.

## Layout

| Area | Size | Height | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start court | 20 × 8 m | 0 m | |
| Jump yard | 38 × 17 m | 0 m | A channel 3 m wide and 1 m deep crosses the whole yard. It has a floor and ramps at both ends, so a missed jump costs 2 s. One block 4 × 4 m at +1 m |
| Gate | 16 × 3 m | 0 m | The only way from the yard to the hall. Checkpoint 1 |
| Trapdoor hall | 40 × 16 m, a grid of 40 cells of 4 × 4 m | 0 m | 27 solid, 7 trapdoors, 3 crumbling, 3 holes. Solid panels make a safe way at the west end and at the east side |
| Undercroft | 20 × 8 m | −4 m | Under the west half of the hall. Three steps of 1 m lead back up to the yard |
| Terrace | 24 × 5 m | +1 m | One step up from the hall. Checkpoint 2 |
| Landing and finish deck | 12 × 4 m, 14 × 7 m | +4 m, +5 m | |

Three ways go from the terrace to the landing:

- **West stair:** three solid pads at +2, +3 and +4 m with 2 m gaps, then a bridge. Safe and long.
- **Centre pillars:** two pillars 2 m wide at +2.2 and +3.3 m. Three precise jumps with rises of 1.2, 1.1 and 0.7 m. The shortest way.
- **East sheet:** a ramp of crumbling tiles 6 × 10 m from +1 to +4 m, then a crumbling bridge 10 m long. The fastest way. The player must not stop.

## Routes

**First run (white).** Jump the channel through the first gem. Jump onto the block for the second gem. Pass the gate. In the hall, go west along the solid south row, roll fast over one trapdoor for a gem, then follow solid panels east. Step onto one crumbling tile for a gem and step back. Take the gem on the east island. Jump up to the terrace, climb the west stair, jump for the last gem and roll onto the finish. Length 192 m, 8 jumps.

**Expert (dashed).** After the gate, cut straight to the trapdoor gem. Then run the middle row east in one line: crumbling tile with gem, trapdoor, a jump over a 4 m hole, island gem. Jump to the terrace and take the east sheet at full speed. Length 131 m, 6 jumps.

## Gems: 6

- 2 in the yard: one above the channel (a jump collects it), one on the block (a jump onto a step).
- 3 in the hall: one on a west trapdoor, one on a centre crumbling tile, one on a solid island in the east. Each loose floor type holds one gem, so the player must touch each type.
- 1 above the landing at +1.2 m. All three ways up meet under it, so no way is forced.

## Clockwork Egg

The egg is in a nook at the east end of the undercroft, behind a pillar. The trapdoor straight ahead of the gate, next to the first hole, drops the marble straight into the nook. A player who falls through any west trapdoor lands in the undercroft, sees the steps to the west and can look east instead. The level's main mistake is the door to its secret.

Hint: the egg glints. The panels have 2 cm gaps between them, and a gold light shows through the gaps round that one trapdoor. A player who stands at the gate and looks at the floor can see it.

## Hazards and objects

Start pad, finish pad, 6 gems, 1 egg, 2 checkpoints, 7 trapdoors, 3 crumbling tiles in the hall, the crumbling sheet and bridge.

## Checkpoints

- **Checkpoint 1** at the gate, after the safe jumps and before the hall.
- **Checkpoint 2** on the terrace, after the hall and before the climb.

A fall in the west half of the hall lands in the undercroft and costs about 6 s. A fall in the east half goes into the void and returns the marble to checkpoint 1 with its gems. Both results teach what a checkpoint does.

## Par and gold

The route bot sets the times (`just suggest-times b02`, 2026-11-21, with the marble tuning of that day).

| Route | Length | Bot time | Rule | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First-run route | 191 m | 32.84 s | par = 32.84 × 1.5 | **49.3 s** |
| Expert route | 145 m | 24.21 s | gold = 24.21 × 1.15 | **27.9 s** |

The estimates before the bot ran were 32 s and 17 s (par 48 s, gold 20 s):

| Route | Length | Arithmetic | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-run route | 192 m | 192 ÷ 10 + 16 turns × 0.5 + 3 stair jumps × 0.9 + 5 jumps × 0.4 | 32 s |
| Expert route | 131 m | 131 ÷ 12 + 7 turns × 0.5 + 6 jumps × 0.4 | 17 s |

The expert route has six sharp turns at the gems (the bot slows to 3.5 m/s in each) and a 90° turn on the crumbling tiles at the top of the east sheet, so the bot is slower than the estimate. Gold needs the expert line: the middle row of the hall, the jump over the hole and the east sheet. The first-run route and the west stair do not reach it.

Speed checks (as designed): a 4 m trapdoor needs 4 ÷ 0.5 = 8 m/s. The 4 m hole needs 4 ÷ 0.75 = 5.3 m/s. The crumbling sheet and bridge are 20 m and each tile holds for 0.5 s, so any speed above 8 m/s is safe. "As built" gives the numbers of the built level.

## Why it is fun

- The hall shows the whole problem at once. The player reads the floor and plans a line.
- Speed changes the map: at 8 m/s the trapdoors become floor. The player feels this the first time a hatch drops behind the marble.
- A fall is a short trip through the undercroft, not a restart.
- The three climbs give a safe way, a precise way and a fast way from the same spot.

## As built

The level script follows the map, with these differences.

| Difference | Reason |
|---|---|
| Each trapdoor, crumbling cell and hole is an opening of 3.6 m in a 0.2 m frame of solid floor. A trapdoor is 3.6 × 3.6 × 0.3 m. A brass frame is set into the floor round each opening | An open door hangs its own length below its hinge. A 4 m door, 0.5 m thick, hangs down to −4.5 m, into the undercroft floor at −4 m. The 3.6 m door hangs down to −3.9 m. So a trapdoor is safe from 7.2 m/s, and the hole in the middle row is 3.6 m |
| The undercroft has an alcove of 4 × 4 m under the north-west trapdoor | On the map that trapdoor is not over the undercroft. The brief says that a fall through any west trapdoor lands in the undercroft |
| The hall has a south wall, 2.6 m high, with one arched gate 3 m wide. Checkpoint 1 is under the arch | On the map a gap of 3 m separates the yard from the hall outside the gate. A marble jumps it from the yard or from the block and skips the gate. A jump from the block does not reach over 2.6 m. A pad 1.5 m wide in a gate 16 m wide catches few players; the routes on the map pass the gate in its middle |
| Checkpoint 2 is three pads on the terrace, one at the foot of each way up | One pad on a terrace 30 m long is not on most lines. With three, a fall from a way up puts the marble back at the foot of that way |
| The terrace is 30 m long (x −14 to +16 m), not 24 m | On the map the foot of the east sheet stands 4 m past the east end of the terrace, over the void. The west stair needs a straight jump from the terrace |
| The west stair goes north, west and north over three pads, then east over a bridge 10 m long. Each jump is a gap of 2 m and a step of 1 m, onto the full side of the next pad | On the map the pads meet corner to corner, so each jump lands on a corner |
| The three undercroft steps are a joined stair with risers of 1 m, along the open west side of the undercroft | The gaps between the steps on the map make a fall after a fall, on the way back from a mistake. On the stair a player is back in the yard in about 6 s |
| The east sheet and its bridge have tiles of 1 m, not 2 m. The bridge is 3 m wide, not 2.8 m. A brass rail frames the sheet and the bridge, and a parapet 1.2 m high stands on the north and east sides of the top | The marble turns 90° onto the bridge at the top of the sheet. A tile breaks 0.5 s after its first touch, so on 2 m tiles a marble below about 5 m/s falls in the turn. On 1 m tiles it stays up above about 3 m/s. A marble at full speed leaves the crest of the sheet through the air and hits the parapet (B1 rule) |
| The yard has a parapet 1.2 m high on its north edge, beside the gate | A full-speed jump over the channel lands 11 m on, past the north edge |
| The crumbling gem is on the north-west tile of its cell, 0.5 m from the solid row to the north. The island gem is 0.8 m north of the middle of its cell. The channel gem floats 0.6 m above the yard. The last gem is 1.7 m south of the finish deck, on the line of the two bridges | On the map the crumbling gem is where four tiles meet, so a touch breaks all four. Near the edge, a player steps onto one tile and off again. The other gems sit on the lines that the routes take |
| The hint for the egg is a gold frame round the egg's trapdoor. The other frames are brass | A door's gap of 1 cm is less than a pixel wide from the gate, and the egg's light is not in the lightmap |

Decoration that the map does not show: the gate arch with a gear wheel on each face and two pillars before it, a gear wheel in the hall wall on each side of the gate, a gear wheel in the face of the landing behind the centre pillars, posts that hold the undercroft ceiling, a gear wheel in the wall of the egg's nook, a gold cog round the start pad, and the clock over the finish. The clock is a brass wheel in a brass ring between two stone piers, with gold hands. Three of its twelve hour balls are missing: the loose hours. The decoration does not collide, except the pillars and the posts.

### Numbers of the built level

- A trapdoor holds a marble at 7.2 m/s or more (3.6 ÷ 0.5). A hall crumbling cell has four tiles of 1.8 m. The sheet and bridge tiles hold a marble that rolls at 3 m/s or more.
- A fall into the void respawns the marble in about 1.6 s. Out of bounds is at −11 m.
- The centre pillars need a jump at 3.5 to 4.5 m/s and a pull back after the landing. Stair pad A to pad B needs 4.5 m/s or more.

### Light

The dawn sun is in the north-west, 12° high. The east sheet climbs north, so it faces away from the sun and is in shade. The south face of the hall wall and of the gate is in shade. The undercroft has light from its open west side; the egg's light shows the nook and its gear wheel.
