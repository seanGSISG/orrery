# B1 Dawn Plaza

Map: `b01.svg`. Ticket: #28. Level script: `levels/beginner/b01.py`. Shared numbers and assumptions: `README.md`.

The map is the approved design. "As built" at the end lists where the level script differs from it, and why.

## Intent

The first level teaches rolling, the camera, gems and the finish. It is an open gem hunt on four floors, not a track. The player chooses the order of the gems. No hazard can push the marble, and almost every fall is a 2 m drop onto another floor.

Flavour: a stone plaza above the cloud sea at dawn, with a brass clock face set into the floor.

## Layout

| Area | Size | Height | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start terrace | 16 × 8 m | 0 m | Opens straight onto the plaza |
| Plaza | 34 × 26 m | 0 m | Clock inlay with a shallow dish (−0.6 m) in the centre. Four pillars to steer round |
| West terrace | 11 × 21 m | +2 m | One 8 m ramp at 14° from the south-west corner of the plaza. Its east edge is open: a 2 m drop onto the plaza |
| Garden | L-shape, 7 to 12 m wide | −2 m | One 10 m ramp from the east side of the plaza. The plaza edge above it is open: a 2 m drop |
| Upper court | 34 × 11 m | +4 m | Grand ramp, 12 m wide, 11 m long, 20° |
| Finish dais | 11 m across | +4 m | North of the court: straight sides and a round end. An armillary sphere on four pillars stands over the finish pad: the horizon ring lies on the pillars, and the meridian ring and the ecliptic ring turn in it on one axle. The player sees it from the start |
| Minute Hand | 1.2 × 9 m beam | +2 to +4 m | A fallen clock hand of gold from the west terrace to the court. No kerb. The slope is 7.5 m long at 15°, with a level heel and a level tip. Its point is a gold diamond set into the floor of the court |
| Hand yard | 12 × 11 m, and 4 × 7 m more behind the terrace | 0 m | A corner of the plaza under the Minute Hand. It catches a fall from each side of the beam |
| Vault | 6.0 × 5.6 m | −2 m | A room under the south-east corner of the court. One arch to the alley, two arched windows to the east |

All outer edges have a 0.3 m brass kerb, except the Minute Hand. An edge above another floor is open. Four edges have a parapet, 1.0 to 2.0 m high, because the marble reaches them through the air: the top of the west ramp, the top of the garden ramp, the south and east sides of the garden, and the two outer sides of the yard.

## Routes

**First run (white).** Roll north into the plaza and take the three gems on the clock face. Go up the west ramp for two gems and come back down. Go down the garden ramp for two gems and come back up. Climb the grand ramp, take the last gem and roll onto the finish. Length 248 m.

**Expert (dashed).** A single loop with no way back. Drop off the open east edge into the garden. Take both garden gems and leave by the garden ramp. Cross the clock face through three gems. Climb the west ramp, take two gems, and cross the Minute Hand to the court. Length 168 m. The drop saves one ramp trip. The Minute Hand saves the return across the plaza and the grand ramp.

## Gems: 8

- 3 on the clock face (centre of the dish, 3 o'clock, 9 o'clock). The player sees them from the start pad. The dish shows how a slope moves the marble. The clock shows six o'clock, and its minute hand is missing: it is the beam.
- 2 on the west terrace and 2 in the garden. They pull the player onto each side floor.
- 1 on the upper court, next to the top of the Minute Hand.
- No gem needs a jump. Each side floor has gems at its far end, so the order of the floors is a real choice.

## Clockwork Egg

The garden strip goes north behind the ramp wall and turns west into an alley along the foot of the court wall. An arch at the west end of the alley opens north into a vault under the upper court. The egg is in the vault. The player cannot see the alley from the ramp: the ramp points south and the wall hides the strip. A player who turns the camera in the garden sees the strip and the last garden gem, which sits halfway to the alley. A player at the north-east kerb of the plaza can also look down into the alley: the arch is in view across the gap, and the egg glints inside. The egg rewards the camera lesson.

## Hazards and objects

Start pad, finish pad, 8 gems, 1 egg. No hazards. No power-ups.

## Checkpoints

None. A fall off the Minute Hand lands in the hand yard, 2 to 4 m below, and costs about 5 s. A fall over a kerb puts the marble back on the start pad in under 2 s, with its gems: out of bounds is 11 m below the plaza.

## Par and gold

The route bot sets the times (`just suggest-times b01`, 2026-11-21, with the marble tuning of that day). Run it again when the tuning changes (#19).

| Route | Length | Bot time | Rule | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First-run route | 248 m | 34.2 s | par = 34.2 × 1.5 | **51.3 s** |
| Expert route | 168 m | 22.2 s | gold = 22.2 × 1.15 | **25.6 s** |

The estimates before the bot ran were 27 s and 18 s (par 41 s, gold 21 s). The bot slows to about 3.5 m/s at each sharp turn, so its times are longer.

Gold needs the expert line: the drop into the garden and the Minute Hand. A better gem order alone does not reach it.

## Why it is fun

- The first view is a wide floor with gems in three directions. The player picks one.
- The dish, the ramps and the 2 m drop teach momentum without a penalty.
- The Minute Hand is in view from the plaza. It asks a question that the player can answer on run two.
- The route order is a small puzzle. A ghost of the best run shows the player how much time the order is worth.
- The armillary sphere marks the finish from the first second. The grand ramp throws a fast marble onto the court.

## As built

The level script follows the map, with these differences.

| Difference | Reason |
|---|---|
| The west terrace is 11 m wide, not 10 m | The map leaves a 1 m gap between the terrace and the plaza. The terrace wall fills it |
| The hand yard goes 4 m further west, north of the terrace | The foot of the Minute Hand is west of the yard on the map. A fall from its west side had no floor below |
| The finish dais has straight sides, not a neck | The neck made two kerb pockets that caught the marble on its way to the finish |
| The Minute Hand has a level heel and a level tip | A beam with square ends, laid across the two edges, made a step at each end |
| Four parapets replace the kerb | In the play-check a marble at top speed flew over the 0.3 m kerb at the top of the west ramp. The flight arithmetic gives the same result at the top of the garden ramp, after the drop into the garden and after a roll off the north edge of the terrace |
| The vault has two windows in its east wall | With one arch the room was black |
| The start pad, the finish pad and five gems have a gold ring in the floor | The start pad has no model of its own. A ring shows where each gem is from a floor above |

Decoration that the map does not show: four slim pillars with brass balls on the plaza, the armillary sphere on the dais, a brass gear wheel in the south wall of the court on each side of the grand ramp, and a row of brass gear teeth below each outer edge. The decoration does not collide, except the pillars.

### Light

The camera looks along +y at the start, and a level cannot change that. So north on the map is +y, and the dawn sun (north-west, 12° high) is ahead of the start and on its left.

- The floors are in the sun, and the shadows of the pillars point at the player.
- The south wall of the court and the grand ramp are in shade. The lightmap lights them from the sky and from the plaza.
- The garden is in the shade of the plaza and of the garden wall. The alley and the vault are the darkest places of the level.
- The shadow of the north-west pillar crosses the centre of the dish: the pillar, the dish centre and the south-east pillar are on one line with the sun.
